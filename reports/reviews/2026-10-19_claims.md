# Orphan claims, motor bodily injury, and 2024 development

Prepared read-only against `~/Projects/iaa-hackathon-2026` (no files in the repo were
changed). Scripts and intermediate CSVs are in `/tmp/iaa-staging/work/1019/claims/`.

## 1. Orphan claims

**Definition used.** A claim is an orphan if its `loss_date` does not fall inside any
policy-period row for its `policy_id` and `line`. There is no case of a claim whose
`policy_id` is entirely absent from the policy file, and no case of multiple matching
rows (the `matched_multiple` count is zero).

**Size.** 410 of 161,439 non-repudiated claims are orphans (0.254% of claims), carrying
$1,721,819 of net cost against $746,052,496 total (0.231% of cost).

| Line | Orphan claims | Orphan net cost | Share of (matched + orphan) line cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | 294 | $1,116,562 | 0.261% |
| Buildings | 50 | $431,488 | 0.174% |
| Contents | 66 | $163,796 | 0.245% |

By loss year: 271 orphans in 2020, 48 in 2021, 47 in 2022, 44 in 2023, none in 2024.
Because the chain-ladder triangles only use claims from ay 2021 onward, 139 of the 410
orphans (those from 2021-2023) are in scope for the development method; the 271 from
2020 are filtered out before they reach the triangles.

**Mechanism.** Every one of the 410 orphans has the same shape: `loss_date` falls
before the earliest policy-period row on file for that `policy_id` and `line`. None
fall after the last row (no post-cancellation claims) and none belong to a policy with
no rows at all. The gap between the loss date and the first row's start is 31 days at
both the 25th and 50th percentile, and up to 499 days at the extreme. This resolves the
open item in `reports/data_quality.md` (finding 4): the claims are not data errors or
cancellations; they are losses on a policy whose own history runs further back than the
2021-2024 extract captures.

**Where orphans go today.**

- `target.py`'s `match_claims`/`reported_targets` excludes every orphan from the row
  targets: none of the $1.72m reaches any row, line total, or anchor.
- `ultimate.py`'s `delay_cdfs` and `freq_sev_adjustment` both read `data.claims()`
  directly rather than the matched set, filtered only by `status != "repudiated"` and
  `ay >= 2021`. The 139 orphans dated 2021-2023 are therefore present when the
  chain-ladder triangles and the motor bodily injury severity base are built, while
  being absent from every row's target. This is an inconsistency, not a treatment
  decision.

**Effect measured.** Dropping the 139 in-scope orphans from the triangle inputs moves
every (line, peril group) completion fraction by at most 0.1-0.2 percentage points
(count and cost triangles both). The motor bodily injury 2021 severity base (used by
the freq_sev method) moves by -0.04% (two orphan BI claims in 2021, net $24,176, against
roughly 2,100 claims used in the mean). Added back at face value with no development,
orphan cost is 0.17%-0.26% of each line's total developed cost (`y_ult`). All of these
are well under the 0.002 noise floor the harness uses to decide whether a change is
real.

**What to change.** Nothing before the Thursday freeze: the effect is immaterial at
every point checked. For Part 2, make `delay_cdfs` and `freq_sev_adjustment` read the
same matched-claim population that `target.py` uses for row targets, so the triangle
inputs and the row targets are drawn from the same claims. This touches the frozen
`ultimate.py`, changes the dataset fingerprint, and forces a rebuild and a TabM
re-tag, so it is listed as a deferred owner item (3.40) rather than an immediate fix.

| 2024 | 20, $3,699,435 | 2, $1,008,500 | 1, $602,050 |

The single largest claim in the book is `CL30973963`: loss 25 June 2023, reported 16
December 2023 (6 months' delay), status open, net $11,894,277. This is the claim
`experiment.py` refers to in its comments ("one 11.9m bodily-injury claim sits in every
motor split"). The next-largest claims are an order of magnitude smaller: $1,118,868 in
2021 and $524,359 in 2022.

**2021 vs 2022.** Raw mean severity differs by 3.4% (21,442 vs 22,173); with the top 1%
of each year excluded, by 3.6% (19,139 vs 19,851). Neither year carries a claim anywhere
near the size of the 2023 one. 2021 is a reasonable, unremarkable base year on severity
grounds; the config's reason for choosing it (most mature reporting cohort) stands on
its own.

**2023 is the distorted year.** Its raw mean severity (23,287) is the highest of the
four years, entirely on the strength of the one claim: with the top 1% excluded, 2023's
mean severity (15,444) is the lowest of 2021-2024, consistent with the data-quality
finding that younger cohorts report lower severity for a given delay once the case
reserve has not yet caught up.

**Report-delay cohort (mean severity):**

| Loss year | 0-3mo | 3-6mo | 6-12mo | 12-24mo | 24-36mo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $21,131 | $20,833 | $19,371 | $25,972 | $31,582 |
| 2022 | $20,804 | $22,304 | $22,355 | $25,033 | $23,619 |
| 2023 | $17,835 | $16,981 | $37,855 | $18,373 | - |
| 2024 | $17,768 | $19,193 | $15,276 | - | - |

The 6-12 month cohort of 2023 ($37,855) is the only one out of line with its neighbours
in either direction; it is the cohort that contains the $11.89m claim. Every other cell
sits within the same range across years. Open share by loss year (10.5%, 15.0%, 27.8%,
49.4%, 79.8% for 2020-2024) rises smoothly with maturity and shows nothing year-specific.

**Base-year sensitivity.** Using the count chain ladder's ultimate claim counts
(2,148 / 2,204 / 2,180 / 2,096 for 2021-2024) and trending severity at 3.5% a year:

| Loss year | Plain cost chain ladder (no override) | freq_sev, base 2021 | freq_sev, base 2022 | Applied in pipeline today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $46,242,173 | $46,067,090 | $46,025,940 | $46,242,173 |
| 2022 | $49,147,496 | $48,905,170 | $48,861,490 | $49,147,496 |
| 2023 | $51,338,527 | $50,083,980 | $50,039,250 | $51,338,527 |
| 2024 | $37,874,925 | $49,832,809 | $49,788,300 | $49,832,809 |

Switching the base year from 2021 to 2022 moves every year's freq_sev figure by less
than 1%. Base year is not the lever that matters.

**What matters instead: the uncapped claim and the floor.** The freq_sev rescaling
factor is `k = max(1.0, ult_count x trended_severity / chain_ladder_ultimate)` - it can
only push a year's figure up, never down. For 2023, the plain chain-ladder ultimate
(51.34m, inflated by the one claim) already exceeds the freq_sev-implied value (50.08m),
so `k` floors at 1.0 and the chain-ladder's inflated figure is the one applied,
unchanged. Capping the claim before developing it removes the inflation:

| Cap on the one claim | 2023 chain-ladder ultimate |
|---|---|
| None (today) | $51,338,527 |
| $1,000,000 | $39,636,292 |
| $500,000 | $39,099,210 |
| $250,000 | $38,830,669 |
| $100,000 | $38,669,545 |

Capped at any of these levels, the chain-ladder figure falls below the freq_sev value
(≈50.08m), so the freq_sev floor would then determine 2023's level instead - a cleaner,
trend-consistent figure in line with the other three years.

`configs/market_dev.yaml` already has a slot for this
(`large_loss.caps.motor_bodily_injury`) but it is set to `null`; no cap is implemented
anywhere in `target.py`, `ultimate.py`, or `experiment.py` today, despite backlog item
2.11 describing a cap-with-reload retry.

**Materiality for the score.** This one training row (`y_ult` ≈ $12,776,398 against a
pooled motor-2023 row mean of $475) accounts for 6.8% of fold A's entire motor null
deviance - the denominator the harness divides by to compute motor D² for that fold.
One row sets most of how forgiving or strict fold A's motor score is.

**What to change.** Implement the cap-and-reload for motor bodily injury: cap each
claim's net cost at a chosen threshold (the table above suggests $500k-$1m is enough to
remove the distortion without touching ordinary large claims), reload the capped excess
across the line/year cohort by exposure so the year's total cost is unchanged, and
develop the capped-and-reloaded series. This is a frozen-target change (owner decision,
backlog 3.37). Leave `base_year` at 2021.

**Motor bodily injury's LDF of 2.41 decomposes into two factors:** a plain
chain-ladder completion of 54.6% reported (LDF 1.83), on top of which the freq_sev
method applies a further factor `k = 1.316`, because count-chain-ladder ultimate
count times trended severity exceeds the plain chain-ladder figure for 2024. (For 2023,
by contrast, `k = 1.0`: the floor binds and the year is carried entirely by the chain
ladder, including its outlier - see section 2.) BI reports slowly: 7.6% of ultimate
cost is known within the loss month, 21.6% within one month, 65.5% within six months,
87% within a year. A 2024 loss month is, on average, about 6 months old at the
valuation date, so more than a third of the year's BI cost is still unseen.

BI's developed loss cost per exposure *falls* from 2023 to 2024 (257.10 to 249.43),
despite a +3.5% severity trend assumption. This is mostly an artifact of section 2: a
2023 figure built the same way as 2024 (freq_sev only, no outlier) is 250.68
($50,083,980 / 199,681.85 exposure) - essentially flat against 2024's 249.43. BI did
not get cheaper in 2024; 2023 looks expensive because of one claim.

**As-if backtest.** To test whether the development method is trustworthy at the
maturity 2024 now carries, each loss year was re-projected using only information that
would have been available 12 months after it ended (report dates on or before
31 December 2023 for loss year 2023 - the same 0-to-11-month maturity window 2024 sits
in today), then compared against the same loss months' position a year later, once more
mature:

| Line | Group | Projected at ~0-11mo maturity | Known ~12 months later | Error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buildings | Accidental damage | 1,129,995 | 1,130,429 | 0.0% |
| Buildings | Water | 15,050,790 | 15,044,750 | +0.0% |
| Buildings | Weather | 35,159,440 | 34,897,900 | +0.8% |
| Contents | All | 17,482,640 | 17,596,460 | -0.6% |
| Motor | Other | 69,793,360 | 69,752,210 | +0.1% |
| Buildings | Fire | 13,744,620 | 14,135,250 | -2.8% |
| Motor | Bodily injury (plain chain ladder) | 69,932,330 | 51,230,940 | +36.5% |

Five of seven groups land within 3% of their more mature figure: the chain-ladder
method, applied at the exact maturity 2024 has today, is sound for buildings and
contents and for motor's non-BI perils. Motor bodily injury's *plain* chain ladder
misses by 36.5% at this maturity - direct confirmation of data-quality finding 3, and
the reason freq_sev exists rather than a cost chain ladder alone.

Running the same test for freq_sev on 2023, using only information known at end of 2023
(an as-if count chain ladder gives an ultimate count of 2,250; 2021 severity trended two
years gives $22,969; the product is $51,680,288), lands close to today's pipeline value
for 2023 (51,338,527, +0.7%), but about 8% above the now-more-mature reported figure for
2023 ($47,807,364, 97.4% complete). freq_sev runs a little high at this maturity -
nowhere near the plain chain ladder's 36.5% to 46.3% miss, but not exact either, and
worth keeping in mind as a small conservative bias rather than treating the method as
settled.

**Month-level chain ladder vs. the row-level sum the pipeline actually uses, 2024:**

| Line | Group | Month-level ultimate | Row-level ultimate (pipeline) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | Bodily injury (plain CL) | 37,056,154 | 37,874,925 | +2.2% |
| Motor | Other | 73,185,672 | 72,965,363 | -0.3% |
| Buildings | Weather | 37,819,958 | 38,038,553 | +0.6% |
| Buildings | Water | 17,932,984 | 17,421,153 | -2.9% |
| Buildings | Fire | 16,137,378 | 16,174,025 | +0.2% |
| Buildings | Accidental damage | 1,323,228 | 1,317,907 | -0.4% |
| Contents | All | 18,183,133 | 18,095,606 | -0.5% |

All seven groups agree within 3%. Averaging completion over a row's calendar months,
rather than developing at the loss-month level directly, is not the source of any
problem seen here; buildings/water's -2.9% gap is the largest and still well inside
normal rounding from aggregation order.

**Buildings: four peril groups, four different stories, same direction.** All four
buildings groups show 2024 ultimate growth above the config's single 6.5% trend
(weather +9.3%, water +16.2%, fire +14.5%, accidental damage +16.6%). Decomposed by
claim count and severity:

- **Fire** (2024: 309 claims, mean severity $50,894; 2023: 279 claims, $50,509): the
  rise is a genuine count increase in a small book (under 300-310 claims a year), not a
  severity or development effect.
- **Escape of water** (mean severity $5,130 in 2021 rising to $6,489 in 2024, roughly
  flat claim counts around 2,500-2,570 a year): this is a multi-year severity trend
  that has run 8-12% a year since 2021, not a 2024-only event. The single pooled 6.5%
  buildings trend understates this peril specifically, every year, not just the most
  recent one.
- **Accidental damage** (counts 877/1,010/967/989, severity $1,176-$1,286, 2021-2024): a
  book of under 1,000 claims a year with no clear direction; the 2024 figure is inside
  normal sampling noise for a book this size.
- **Weather** is the one real oddity. Reported cost for 2024 actually *fell* 2.6% from
  2023 ($33,756,648 vs $34,718,434 for the comparable loss months), while the developed
  ultimate *rose* 9.3% ($38,038,553 vs $34,804,198), entirely from an 11% IBNR load. The
  backtest above shows this method is not biased at this maturity (+0.8% error on the
  equivalent test), so the honest reading is that 2024's weather losses reported slowly
  rather than being genuinely light, and the development method is restoring, not
  inventing, the missing part.

**Partial-year exposure.** The share of training rows that are full-year policies is
stable from 2022 on (motor 59.5-59.6%, buildings 64.4-64.6%, contents 61.9-62.1% every
year 2022-2024); 2021 alone runs higher (75-82%) because it is the first year of the
extract. 2024 carries no unusual amount of partial-year exposure.

**A caution on the trend check itself.** The dashboard's own test ("developed 2024
should sit on the 2021-2023 trend... within 2%") is not fully independent for motor:
motor's 3.0% trend assumption was fitted on data that includes the 2024 point, and that
point is itself built from freq_sev's own fixed 3.5% severity trend assumption for BI.
The two numbers are not drawn from separate evidence.

**What to change.** Buildings, contents, and motor's non-BI perils check out: the
backtest and the month-vs-row comparison both confirm the method at this maturity.
Motor bodily injury's volatility is real and already handled by the right method
(freq_sev, not a cost chain ladder); the fix that would actually move the 2024 and 2023
numbers is the same large-loss cap described in section 2, implemented together with
it. Consider a peril-specific trend for escape of water rather than folding its faster
severity trend into the single buildings-wide 6.5% figure.
