# Feature review: cross-line leakage, premium reliance, email domain

Read-only review of `~/Projects/iaa-hackathon-2026` against the champion at commit
`ffb3e8b` (HEAD, 2026-10-19 00:24 local). The loop was left untouched; no file in the
repo was read or written except via `git show`. All numbers below come from scripts
under `/tmp/iaa-staging/work/1019/features/` that reimport the frozen `iaa` modules
(`data`, `features`, `target`, `ultimate`, `harness.split`) and refit a lighter version
of the champion: single seed, LightGBM only (no EBM blend, no 3-seed bag), 500 rounds
at learning rate 0.05 instead of 1,200 at 0.03 (matches `scripts/feature_study.py`'s own
budget), fold B only (train 2021-23, valid 2024). Motor keeps the champion's
BI-Poisson-plus-other-Tweedie split; buildings and contents are the LightGBM member
only, not the 50/50 EBM blend. Feature selection, the credibility encoder and the
monotone constraints are copied verbatim from `experiment.py` (git HEAD). Because this
is one seed, not three, single-run deltas under about 0.003 are not distinguishable
from noise; where that matters it is stated plainly and, for one case (email domain on
buildings), confirmed with extra seeds.

This builds on `docs/pipeline_review.md` (2026-10-18), which already covers written
premium's timing and redundancy in detail. Where a number repeats here it is
cross-checked against a champion-style model rather than the review's lighter test
model; where it does not repeat, this review goes further (the cross-line claim
history, the domain-level proxy test, the engineered-feature check).

## 1. Cross-line feature leakage

**Short answer: no driving or vehicle detail reaches buildings or contents, and no
buildings- or contents-specific field reaches motor. The leakage runs the other way,
through two channels that sit above the line-specific policy tables: the address
record (which carries buildings-style hazard detail onto every motor row) and the
customer record (which carries a driving-record field onto every property row, and
pools claim history across all three lines under one customer).**

### 1.1 Why the line-specific tables don't leak

`policies_motor.parquet`, `policies_buildings.parquet` and `policies_contents.parquet`
are three separate tables. `features.build()` stacks them into one frame by
concatenation, so a motor row's `construction_type`, `sum_insured` and so on are
genuinely absent (NaN), not zero or blanked after the fact. `experiment._feature_cols`
drops any column that is entirely NaN for the line being fit
(`cols = [c for c in cols if df[c].notna().any()]`, checked on that line's own training
rows). Checked directly against the champion's per-line feature lists: zero vehicle or
driver fields reach buildings or contents; zero buildings-only fields
(`construction_type`, `occupancy`, `days_unoccupied`, `smoke_alarms`, `mortgage`) reach
motor or contents; zero contents-only fields (`tenure`, `security_level`,
`high_value_items`, `works_from_home`, `lodgers`, `bicycle_cover`) reach motor or
buildings. `sum_insured` and `accidental_damage_cover` are not a leak either: both
buildings and contents have their own column of that name, and concatenation keeps
each row's own line's value, never another line's.

### 1.2 Where the leakage actually is

**Address table reaching motor.** Every motor row carries an `address_id` and is
joined to the same `addresses.parquet` and `weather.parquet` tables as buildings and
contents. `region` and `urban_density` are legitimate location descriptors for a motor
risk (traffic density, theft and collision exposure). The other 12 address fields and
all 7 weather fields have no motor-rating rationale and reach the motor model anyway,
because they are address-level, not line-level, and `notna().any()` only checks for
missingness, not relevance:

`flood_zone`, `distance_to_river_km`, `elevation_m`, `soil_type`, `property_type`,
`roof_type`, `basement`, `trees_within_5m`, `listed_building`, `bedrooms`,
`energy_rating`, `solar_panels`, `year_built`/`property_age`, and `hail_days`,
`wind_exposure_index`, `freezing_days`, `dry_summer_index`, `wildfire_index`,
`rainfall_mm`, `mean_temperature_c`.

Individually, most of these carry close to zero permutation importance on motor in the
study model (`reports/features/feature_study.json`): `elevation_m` -0.0000,
`energy_rating` -0.0001, `distance_to_river_km` -0.0003, `flood_zone` -0.0000. But the
group as a whole is not free: `feature_study.json`'s group-drop test shows motor D2 up
0.0031 if `location_hazard` (region, urban_density, flood_zone,
distance_to_river_km, elevation_m, soil_type) is dropped entirely, and up 0.0018 if
`weather` is dropped, a combined 0.0049, almost a full noise-floor unit, from fields a
motor underwriter would never see. Given `region` and `urban_density` individually
carry real signal (motor gain 961 and 209, the two location fields a motor rater would
actually use), the most defensible reading is that the lift sits mostly in those two,
and the rest of the group rides along as noise the trees occasionally fit, propped up
by LightGBM's `feature_fraction` sampling always finding something to split on in a
large dataset. The champion also applies `flood_zone: +1` as a monotone constraint,
written for buildings and contents, but it binds on motor too, because `flood_zone` is
in motor's feature list.

**Customer table reaching all three lines, including a driving-record field.**
`licence_points` lives in `customers.parquet`, not `policies_motor.parquet`. That is a
driving-record field (penalty points) attached to the person, not the vehicle or the
motor policy, so it is non-null and selected into buildings' and contents' feature
lists exactly as it is into motor's. Its measured effect is small either way (motor
+0.0003, buildings -0.0001, contents +0.0000 permutation), so it is not moving the
property scores, but it is the cleanest single example of the pattern Karl asked
about: a driving detail that reaches home and contents pricing by construction, not by
intent. The champion's monotone constraint `licence_points: +1` binds on buildings and
contents the same way `flood_zone: +1` binds on motor.

**Customer-level claim history pools claims across lines.**
`features.claim_history()` builds `hist_n_customer` / `hist_cost_customer` from
`data.claims()` keyed only on `customer_id`, with no line filter
(`features.py:56-69`). `hist_n_policy` / `hist_cost_policy`, by contrast, key on
`policy_id`, and `policy_id` values do not overlap across lines (checked directly:
zero shared IDs between any pair of the three line tables), so those two are
same-line only. The customer-keyed pair are not. Quantified on fold B rows (own
script, not a model fit):

| Line | Rows with `hist_n_customer` > 0 | Of those, share where a *different* line's claim contributes | Of those, cost share from the *different* line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | 8.75% | 14.4% | 20.4% |
| Buildings | 6.58% | 63.6% | 40.1% |
| Contents | 6.53% | 66.6% | 81.3% |

For contents specifically, when the history feature fires at all, most of the time
(67% of rows) and most of the cost (81%) behind it comes from a motor or buildings
claim, not a contents claim. The aggregate permutation importance of
`hist_n_customer`/`hist_cost_customer` is small on every line (+-0.0001 to +0.0002,
per `feature_study.json`), because only 6.5-8.8% of rows are touched at all, but for
that subset, a customer's motor claim is nudging their buildings or contents price,
and vice versa. Whether that is defensible depends on whether "claims elsewhere in the
household" is a risk factor you want to price on. It is a different question from
pure leakage (it is not the target leaking into its own prediction) and a milder one
than the address/weather case, but it is real and currently undocumented as a design
choice.

**Admissibility finding adjacent to the cross-line question.** Across all three
lines, `marketing_segment`, `region` and `campaign_code` are the top three features by
gain in this review's fits, ahead of every genuine risk factor, including
`written_premium` and `sum_insured`. `docs/pipeline_review.md` already flags
`campaign_code`, `marketing_segment`, `document_preference` and `branch_id` as having
"no risk rationale" (admissibility, not cross-line leakage); this review's numbers
confirm the ranking by this measure. `marketing_segment`
(12 levels) and `campaign_code` (15 levels) are both under the credibility-encoding
threshold and so are split natively, which can inflate gain importance for a
mid-cardinality categorical relative to permutation importance. This is noted for
completeness; it is not re-litigated here since it is already an open item (backlog
2.1, 2.31, 3.35).

### 1.4 What to change

Drop the 12 address-hazard fields and all 7 weather fields from motor's feature list
(keep `region` and `urban_density`); they have no motor-rating rationale, carry
near-zero individual permutation importance on motor, and their combined group-drop
gain (0.0049) is small enough that losing it is not a material score risk. Split
`hist_n_customer`/`hist_cost_customer` into same-line and cross-line components (or
drop the cross-line component) so a customer's claim on one line stops moving the
price on another without an explicit decision to price that way; this is cheap (the
windowing code already exists in `features.claim_history`) and resolves an
undocumented design choice rather than a measured score problem. Treat
`licence_points` on buildings and contents the same way for consistency, even though
its measured effect there is near zero; an auditor will ask why a home insurance
price depends on driving penalty points before they ask about the D2 impact.

## 2. Written premium reliance (buildings, contents)

**Short answer: premium's apparent importance on buildings is a measurement artefact.
A real drop test under the champion's own encoding costs buildings 0.0011 D2 and
costs contents nothing (contents improves by 0.0008), both inside the repo's own
0.002 noise floor. A regression of log(premium) on every other buildings feature gets
R-squared = 0.965 from `flood_zone`, `sum_insured`, `distance_to_river_km`, `region` and
`elevation_m` alone, fields already in the model, so there is very little premium
information left to replace. Engineered interactions do not recover anything; a
single combined variant scored lower than simply dropping premium.**

### 2.1 Quantifying the reliance

| Measure | Buildings | Contents | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permutation importance, study model (`feature_study.json`, native categoricals, no credibility encoder) | 0.0395 | 0.0130 | Largest single-feature permutation value on buildings in that model |
| Gain importance, this review's champion-style fit | 220 (rank 10 of 62, behind marketing_segment, region, campaign_code, energy_rating, distance_to_river_km, elevation_m, construction_type, sum_insured, previous_insurer) | 278 (rank 7 of 63) | Premium is not the top driver once sum_insured, flood/location and marketing fields are present |
| D2, with premium (this review, champion-style, fold B) | 0.1065 | 0.0845 | -- |
| D2, without premium | 0.1054 (-0.0011) | 0.0853 (+0.0008) | Both inside the 0.002 noise floor; contents is *better* without it |
| Pipeline review's independent quick test (two seeds, native categoricals, lr 0.08) | 0.0960 without vs 0.0945 with (+0.0015) | 0.0773 without vs 0.0780 with (-0.0007) | Same direction on buildings, same conclusion on both: inside noise |

The permutation number (0.0395) and the gain number (220, rank 10) disagree sharply
because permutation measures the fitted model's reliance on the feature as it stands,
while a real drop test lets the model re-split on substitutes. `docs/pipeline_review.md`
already made this point for the study model; this confirms it holds under the
champion's own credibility encoding and monotone constraints, not just the lighter
study model.

### 2.2 What premium encodes that the model doesn't already have

Regressing `log(written_premium)` on every other buildings (or contents) feature,
champion-style encoding, single seed, fold B:

| | Buildings | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| R-squared | 0.965 | 0.931 |
| Top drivers (gain) | flood_zone, sum_insured, distance_to_river_km, region, elevation_m, marketing_segment, rainfall_mm, bedrooms, campaign_code | sum_insured, region, marketing_segment, dry_summer_index, campaign_code, rainfall_mm, urban_density, wildfire_index, distance_to_river_km |
| Gini of the residual against this year's claims | 0.012 | 0.003 |

Two readings follow. First, premium's risk content is almost entirely redundant with
fields already in the model: `flood_zone`, `sum_insured`, `distance_to_river_km`,
`region`, `elevation_m` for buildings; `sum_insured`, `region`, the weather fields for
contents. There is very little unique risk signal left over: the residual's Gini
against same-year claims (0.012 / 0.003) is close to zero, nowhere near a field worth
keeping for its ranking power alone. Second, a material share of what premium
*does* encode beyond pure risk is the insurer's own commercial segmentation
(`marketing_segment`, `campaign_code`, `discount_applied`, `previous_insurer`), which
is not a risk factor at all; it is evidence the incumbent's price reflects who they
decided to discount, not only what the property costs to insure.

### 2.3 Engineered alternatives, prioritised

Given finding 2.2 (premium's unique content is nearly zero once flood/location and
sum insured are already in the model), the realistic expectation for any engineered
replacement is small. Tested and proposed, in priority order:

1. **Tested, did not help.** One combined variant on buildings without premium:
   `sum_insured` in 8 exposure-weighted bands, `construction_type` (case-normalised)
   crossed with a 5-band `property_age`, `flood_zone x rainfall_mm`,
   `soil_type`-weighted-by-`trees_within_5m` `x dry_summer_index` (subsidence
   mechanics), and `(voluntary_excess + compulsory_excess) / sum_insured`. All five
   get used by the trees (gain 169-272, comparable to existing top-20 features) but
   the combined D2 (0.1019) is *below* both the no-premium baseline (0.1054) and the
   full baseline (0.1065), consistent with `docs/pipeline_review.md`'s backlog item
   2.2 (full-model peril-mechanics interactions, "tried: below best"). At num_leaves
   15 the trees already approximate these interactions from the base fields; explicit
   terms mostly compete with them for split budget rather than adding information.
2. **Worth a real (3-seed, both-fold) test, not yet run**: use `written_premium / sum_insured`
   instead of `written_premium`. That removes the inflation drift and the direct
   sum_insured correlation (0.57 buildings, 0.91 contents per the pipeline review)
   without discarding the admin-cost component of premium. This is backlog 2.28, already open; this review's regression finding
   (R-squared 0.965 from fields already present) is independent support for prioritising it
   over new interaction terms.
3. **Not worth building**: further peril-mechanics interactions beyond the one tested.
   The base fields are present, the trees already use them, and the one variant
   tested using them in combination scored worse, not better.

### 2.4 What to change

Drop `written_premium` from buildings and contents (confirms backlog 2.26/2.27 under
an independent champion-style replication); the cost is inside the noise floor on
both, and contents is measurably better without it. Keep it on motor, where
`docs/pipeline_review.md` found a real experience-rating signal: next year's
premium ranks this year's claims better than its own year's premium does. That means
the incumbent re-rates on claims, including ones unreported at renewal, a different
and larger effect than the redundant-information story on buildings and contents. Do not spend further time on peril-mechanics interaction engineering for
buildings/contents; the regression test shows there is very little premium content
left to replace, and the one variant tried confirms it.

## 3. Email domain

**Short answer: it is noise. Cramer's V between `email_domain` and six candidate
proxies is 0.003-0.008 (0 = independent); there is no detectable relationship to
age, credit band, occupation, tenure, region or channel. The one feature-drop result
that looked real (buildings D2 -0.0057 at seed 0) reverses sign under a second seed
and a `feature_fraction = 1.0` control (-0.0039 to +0.0057 across six reruns), which
is the definition of noise, not signal.**

### 3.1 Per-domain portfolio stats

Five domains, near-uniform (~109,000-109,400 customers each, 0% missing on
buildings/contents; motor email_domain is ~10.0% missing in both train and test,
stable across years, a real feature of that table, not a join bug). One-way exposure,
claims and relativity, 2021-23 vs 2024 (`reports/features/feature_study.json`):

| Line | Domain | Exposure 2021-23 | Relativity 2021-23 | Relativity 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | inboxly | 106,538 | 0.962 | 1.035 |
| Motor | mailone | 105,018 | 0.953 | 1.006 |
| Motor | netbox | 105,851 | 1.137 | 0.981 |
| Motor | other | 164,723 | 0.992 | 0.971 |
| Motor | posthub | 105,241 | 0.960 | 0.959 |
| Buildings | inboxly | 88,060 | 0.945 | 1.097 |
| Buildings | other | 88,188 | 1.061 | 1.107 |
| Buildings | posthub | 88,461 | 1.051 | 1.003 |
| Contents | posthub | 88,359 | 1.041 | 1.072 |

(full table in `feature_study.json`). No domain holds a stable rank across both
periods on any line; `netbox` runs hottest on motor in 2021-23 (relativity 1.137) and
coolest in 2024 (0.981): the opposite of what a real rating factor would do, since a real
factor should hold its rank across periods.

### 3.2 Is it one or two domains, and does dropping email domain cost anything

Fitting the champion-style model *without* `email_domain` and comparing its
prediction to actual 2024 cost, by domain (fold B valid, single seed):

| Line | Domain | A/E vs the no-domain model |
|---|---|---|
| Buildings | inboxly | 1.105 |
| Buildings | other | 1.099 |
| Buildings | mailone / netbox / posthub | 1.01-1.01 |
| Contents | netbox | 0.912 |
| Contents | posthub | 1.066 |
| Motor | other | 0.955 |
| Motor | inboxly | 1.045 |

On buildings this does look like two domains (`inboxly`, `other`) running 10% hot
against a model that cannot see domain at all, exactly Karl's question. But this A/E
split comes from the same single-seed buildings fit whose overall D2 swung
+0.0057/-0.0039 just from reseeding (below). A segment-level split inherited from a
fit that unstable is not evidence of a stable two-domain effect; it is more likely the
same single-seed noise concentrating in two of five categories by chance. This would
need a 3-seed-plus-both-fold version of the same table before treating any one
domain as the driver; not done here, flagged as backlog 2.44.

### 3.3 Why the single buildings result looked real, and why it isn't

| Confirmation run | feature_fraction | Seed | D2 with domain | D2 without | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 0.8 | 0 | 0.1065 | 0.1008 | +0.0057 |
| Reseed 1 | 0.8 | 1 | 0.1008 | 0.0986 | +0.0022 |
| Reseed 2 | 0.8 | 2 | 0.1018 | 0.1001 | +0.0017 |
| No-resampling-shift control | 1.0 | 0 | 0.1017 | 0.0975 | +0.0042 |
| No-resampling-shift control | 1.0 | 1 | 0.0961 | 0.1000 | **-0.0039** |
| No-resampling-shift control | 1.0 | 2 | 0.1021 | 0.1010 | +0.0011 |

The with-domain D2 alone ranges from 0.0961 to 0.1065 across six reruns that differ
only in seed and `feature_fraction`, a spread of 0.0104, four times the repo's own
measured single-seed noise sd (0.0026) and larger than the delta being tested. One of
the six reruns (feature_fraction 1.0, seed 1) has the *no-domain* model scoring
higher. This is consistent with (not proof of, but consistent with) `email_domain`
simply shifting LightGBM's `feature_fraction` random draw for every tree when it is
removed from the candidate list; a feature with zero real signal can still move the
score through that channel alone. `feature_study.json`'s permutation importance for
`email_domain` is -0.0004 (buildings), -0.0003 (contents), +0.0001 (motor), all
indistinguishable from zero, and negative on the two property lines, meaning shuffling
it (not even removing it) improved the study model slightly. That is the more
reliable number; the headline -0.0057 buildings drop does not survive a second seed.

### 3.4 What it would be a proxy for, if anything

Cramer's V, `email_domain` against candidate proxies (customer-level fold B rows,
deduplicated to one row per customer):

| Candidate | Cramer's V |
|---|---|
| occupation | 0.0081 |
| region | 0.0056 |
| channel | 0.0040 |
| customer_age (banded) | 0.0038 |
| years_at_address (banded) | 0.0035 |
| credit_band | 0.0027 |
| customer_tenure (banded) | 0.0026 |

0 is statistical independence; every value here is close enough to 0 that
`email_domain` is not standing in for age, credit band, occupation, tenure, region or
channel in this data. Combined with the near-uniform exposure split (~20% per domain,
by construction) and the rank-flipping relativities in 3.1, the most defensible
reading is that `email_domain` here is not a real-world demographic signal at all:
it looks like an uncorrelated categorical the generator attached to each customer, and
the cases where a model appears to price on it are the model fitting finite-sample
noise in a 5-level category, not a stable segment effect and not a proxy for anything
protected or commercial.

### 3.5 What to change

Drop `email_domain` from all three lines. The score cost is indistinguishable from
zero under a proper multi-seed check (the repo's own 3-seed bag already absorbs this;
a single-seed read, which is what produced the -0.0057 buildings number, should not be
used to argue for keeping it). This converges with backlog 2.31's instinct but
narrows it: that item bundles `email_domain` with `credit_band`, `occupation` and
`marital_status`, three fields that are not shown to be noise here (`prior_claims_5y`
and `credit_band` carry real permutation signal on every line, Section 1.3).
`email_domain` is the one field in that group with no proxy relationship to anything
and a permutation importance statistically at zero; it can be dropped on its own
grounds without waiting on the other three.
