# Fairness and proxy review: driver/customer age, marital status, occupation, credit band, household size, geography

Read-only review of `~/Projects/iaa-hackathon-2026` against `src/iaa/experiment.py` at
HEAD (`73ea481`). The working tree is dirty (the loop is mid-iteration on a change to
`_bi_level`/`_bi_fs_ratio`, backlog's BI frequency-severity floor); that diff does not
touch `_feature_cols`, `LINE_DROP`, `PRUNED` or `MONOTONE`, so the feature membership
below is HEAD's, confirmed by importing `iaa.experiment` directly rather than reading
the file. No repo file was written; the harness CLI was not run; `results.tsv` was not
touched. All fitting and scoring here uses the cached dataset parquet
(`data/cache/dataset_*.parquet`, fingerprint matches current `data/target/ultimate/
features.py`) via `iaa.harness.dataset()`.

**Model used in this review.** A single-seed LightGBM Tweedie (power 1.5, 350 rounds,
learning rate 0.05, `num_leaves=15`, `min_data_in_leaf=2000`, `num_threads=3`), fold B
(train 2021-23, valid 2024, the fold the leaderboard primary weights double), with the
current `MONOTONE` constraints and `HIGH_CARD_MIN=15` credibility encoding copied
verbatim from `experiment.py`. Motor training `y` is capped at 1,000,000 x exposure,
mirroring `BI_CAP`, so the one 11.9m bodily-injury claim does not inject tree noise
into the feature-toggle deltas below; this review's motor model has no separate BI /
other-perils split, so its own D2 is not comparable to `results.tsv` or to
`reports/features/model_card.json` (which is the champion's permutation-importance
pass on the pre-pruning superset of features -- cited below for corroboration, not as
the primary number). Single-seed noise, measured with one reseed of the base fit on
each line: motor 0.0017, buildings 0.0009, contents 0.0012 D2. A delta smaller than
about 0.002 on one line is not distinguishable from this noise.

**What this review can and cannot speak to.** Sex, race, ethnicity and religion are
absent from the dataset and from this review's candidate list (Q23: banned in GB,
correctly excluded upstream). Age, marital status, occupation and credit band are the
four fields Karl named as acceptable and indicative of behaviour; household size and
geography (region, urban_density) are added here as the two other plausible proxy
candidates in the schema. Proxy strength is therefore measured only among fields GB
law already permits -- there is no absolute-discrimination question left to answer,
only the Equality Act's "relevant data" test for age, and the FCA Consumer Duty /
proxy-scrutiny test for marital status, occupation and credit band.

The driver_age and customer_age/policyholder_age rows are the clearest case of the two
measures disagreeing on magnitude rather than direction: permutation importance
shuffles the column while holding its proxy fixed, so it credits driver_age with the
variance it shares with `years_licensed` (or customer_age with what it shares with
policyholder_age); a refit without the column lets the trees reallocate to the proxy
and shows the true, near-zero marginal cost of the column itself. Motor's three
borderline "add" numbers (marital_status, occupation, household_size, all 0.0022-0.0029,
against a measured single-seed noise of 0.0017) are reported as-is rather than
rounded to zero, but are not strong enough on their own to recommend reinstating any
of them; a confirmation reseed was not run for these three, in line with the existing
reviews' practice of not re-tightening small positive numbers that other evidence
already explains away (here, Section 3's reconstruction results).

## 3. Proxy strength

Spearman for number-number) of each sensitive field against every other feature in
that line's current model, plus a light single-seed LightGBM (200 rounds,
`num_threads=3`, up to 250k rows) predicting the sensitive field from the line's other
current features, reported against the majority-class base rate (classification) or
R2 (ages, household size).

Three findings carry weight beyond the table:

**Age is reconstructable, not removable.** `driver_age` <-> `years_licensed` and
`customer_age` <-> `policyholder_age` are, to three decimal places, the same variable.
Dropping the named age field from the feature list (the "unaware" model) does not
remove age's effect on price -- Section 4's discrimination-free check shows the
unaware model's price barely moves from the aware model's, because the correlated
column fully substitutes. Anyone wanting to demonstrate "we don't price on age"
by pointing to an unaware refit would be demonstrating nothing; the model still prices
on age through its proxy. Genuine age-neutral pricing requires the marginalisation
in Section 4, which is expensive (it costs real D2) precisely because age (via either
name) is one of the largest single rating factors in the dataset -- the empirical
form of the "relevant data" the Equality Act's financial-services exception asks for.

**Region is almost entirely a relabelling of the weather fields.** AUC 0.95-0.99
reconstructing region from `freezing_days`/`wildfire_index`/`rainfall_mm`/
`elevation_m` means region carries little information the weather fields don't
already carry on their own, and already legitimately (property/weather exposure
has an obvious rating rationale on every line; `reports/reviews/2026-10-19_features.md`
section 1.2 flags the same weather-group reach into motor). This matters for any
future redress: dropping or marginalising region alone would not materially change
geographic price differences, because the weather group would still reconstruct them
(confirmed directly in Section 4).

**Occupation's proxy risk depends on the line.** On motor and contents, occupation's
strongest correlate is the age field (correlation ratio 0.66) and its reconstruction
accuracy (33-35% against a 19% base rate) is meaningfully above chance -- consistent
with age-coded categories (Retired, Student) doing most of the work. On buildings,
where occupation is actually in the model, reconstruction barely clears the base rate
(20.3% vs 19.4%) and its strongest correlate (years_at_address, 0.16) is weak -- the
occupation signal on buildings looks like real, independent content, not an age
relabel. `household_size` and `urban_density` are the two fields in this review with
no material association with anything and no material reconstruction accuracy; they
are not functioning as proxies for the other four.

## 4. Discrimination-free pricing check

Lindholm, Richman, Tsanakas & Wuthrich, "Discrimination-Free Insurance Pricing" (2022).
For each (line, field) where the field is currently in the model: (a) **aware** price
= the current model's own prediction; (b) **unaware** price = the same model refit
without the field (Section 2's drop variant); (c) **discrimination-free (DF)** price =
the aware model's prediction marginalised over the training, exposure-weighted
portfolio distribution of the field, holding every other feature at the row's own
value (ages: 10 exposure-weighted bins, substituted at the bin's weighted mean;
categoricals: every observed level, weighted by its training exposure share). This is
a discrete approximation to the continuous marginalisation in the paper. D2 values
below are fold B, this review's single-seed model (not comparable to the champion).

| Line | Field | D2 aware | D2 unaware | D2 DF | Sigma(unaware)/Sigma(aware) | Sigma(DF)/Sigma(aware) | Share moved >5%, unaware | Share moved >5%, DF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | driver_age | 0.0762 | 0.0766 | **0.0683** | 0.997 | 0.949 | 67% | 79% |
| Motor | credit_band | 0.0762 | 0.0771 | 0.0759 | 0.993 | 0.999 | 65% | 24% |
| Motor | region+urban_density | 0.0762 | 0.0730 | 0.0747 | 0.987 | 0.998 | 77% | 73% |
| Buildings | occupation | 0.1130 | 0.1132 | 0.1130 | 0.996 | 1.000 | 77% | 40% |
| Buildings | credit_band | 0.1130 | 0.1135 | 0.1133 | 0.990 | 0.999 | 78% | 50% |
| Contents | customer_age | 0.0906 | 0.0906 | 0.0885 | 1.006 | 0.980 | 73% | 74% |
| Contents | customer_age + policyholder_age (joint) | 0.0906 | n/a | **0.0884** | n/a | 0.989 | n/a | 79% |
| Contents | policyholder_age | 0.0906 | 0.0909 | 0.0907 | 1.001 | 0.991 | 72% | 35% |
| Contents | credit_band | 0.0906 | 0.0909 | 0.0911 | 1.008 | 0.998 | 75% | 38% |
| Contents | region+urban_density | 0.0906 | 0.0876 | 0.0871 | 0.991 | 0.997 | 82% | 81% |

**Age is the one field where true discrimination-free treatment has a real,
measurable cost and real distributional consequences.** Motor's D2 falls from 0.0762
to 0.0683 (about 10% relative) under DF marginalisation of driver_age -- far above the
0.0017 noise floor -- while the merely-unaware refit costs nothing (years_licensed
absorbs it). By age band, the DF price falls to 0.57-0.74x the aware price for drivers
under 31 and rises to 1.14-1.21x for drivers 54-69 (`out/step3_df_motor.json`); the
unaware price stays within 0.86-1.02x of aware across every band. Contents shows the
same pattern once both age columns are marginalised together (0.0906 to 0.0884,
individual bands 0.82x to 1.14x), confirming the duplicate-column effect in Section 3
rather than a genuinely smaller age effect on contents. Age-blind pricing, if ever
required, would need the marginalisation step on both age columns at once, would cost
real accuracy, and would shift money from older to younger policyholders -- the exact
cross-subsidy the Equality Act's exception is designed to let an insurer avoid when
the underlying data supports it, which here it does.

**Geography's DF and unaware prices move together, because weather already
substitutes.** On motor and contents, the DF price tracks the unaware price closely
region by region (e.g. motor R05: DF 1.218x, unaware 1.197x) -- consistent with
Section 3's finding that weather fields reconstruct region almost completely.
Marginalising geography alone, with the weather group left in place, barely changes
the geographic spread of prices.

**Credit band and occupation move individual prices without moving the fold-level
score.** Buildings occupation: D2 unchanged to the fourth decimal under DF treatment,
but 40% of policies still move price by more than 5% (occupation's effect is
offsetting across levels, not simply weak). Credit band behaves similarly on all
three lines: D2 cost under DF is inside noise everywhere, but 24-50% of policies move
more than 5%, concentrated in band E (DF ratio 0.86-0.89 of aware on every line,
i.e. band E would be undercharged by 11-14% if credit band's effect were
marginalised away) -- band E is also where the unaware price already differs most
from aware (E's unaware/aware ratio 0.89-0.91 on all three lines), because
`missed_payments_12m` and `payment_method` partly recover what credit_band would
otherwise carry.
