## 1. Verdict in brief

The pipeline already handles the points where competition entries most often fail: targets are developed and trended before any modelling, validation is out of time on two folds, target encoding is fitted inside each training fold, the agent cannot touch the target or the scorer, and every run is logged with its hypothesis and verdict. The material gaps sit in the middle of the document's funnel. There is no redundancy map (the data holds exact duplicates such as driver_age and customer_age), no repeated selection across folds, seeds or target constructions, and no paired confidence interval or double-lift behind any keep decision. The variable study that feeds the open backlog was run on a different model from the champion, on one fold with one seed, and the first item taken from it (2.17) did not hold under the real model. The single deterministic ultimate is used both to train and to score, the development triangle includes the 2024 diagonal, and the trend selection was informed by 2024, so fold B's level is optimistic by construction. On written premium, this review finds no trace of the row's own claims in the premium, but motor renewal premiums are re-rated on prior-year claims that had not yet been reported at the renewal date, and in a quick test the property premium adds nothing beyond noise once the other fields are present. Before Thursday: fix the keep-rule bar and the fingerprint coupling in one owner commit tonight, add the missing guardrail tests, let the loop run the 15 hypotheses in section 5 (5 to 6 hours of loop time with the open backlog), and pick the final submission by a rule declared in advance. Multiple development draws, the reserving replay, a GLM/GAM tariff reconstruction and the full selection-stability matrix belong to Part 2.

## 2. Gap analysis

### 2.1 Written premium: what it is and how to treat it

Written premium is not part of the target. The target is the row's ultimate claim cost; the premium is the price the incumbent insurer charged, fixed at inception and supplied for the 2025 test rows. Karl's concern still holds as a question of circularity: a model that uses the incumbent's price learns corrections to the incumbent's tariff. The document lists this under "Modelling loss ratio by default" (it "imports the current premium algorithm and confounds risk with historical pricing"). Four tests settle the narrower leakage question and size the benefit.

| Test (this review) | Motor | Buildings | Contents | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium relativity by quarter of the period in which the row's single claim occurred (rows with one claim, exposure > 0.9) | 1.128, 1.130, 1.136, 1.125 | 1.215, 1.270, 1.291, 1.242 | 1.030, 1.039, 1.040, 1.041 | No trend with timing. A mid-term re-rate after a claim would give early-quarter claims a higher premium, and that pattern is absent. |
| Change in log premium relativity into year N, by claims in year N (same period) vs claims in year N-1 | +0.6 pp for same-period claims; +8.9 pp for prior-year claims | +0.9 pp; -0.9 pp | -0.1 pp; -0.1 pp | Same-period claims barely move the premium; prior-year claims move motor premium a lot. |
| Gini of claims ranked by premium, consecutive-year pairs: G(prem N → claims N), G(prem N → claims N+1), G(prem N+1 → claims N), G(prem N+1 → claims N+1) | 0.192, 0.142, 0.295, 0.155 | 0.314, 0.320, 0.315, 0.319 | 0.219, 0.218, 0.220, 0.216 | Motor: next year's premium ranks this year's claims best, i.e. renewal re-rating on experience. An insurer could not have known these at the quote. |

"After renewal" means the claim's report_date is on or after the start date of the same policy's next period.

Two more measurements bear on the decision. First, the incumbent price on its own is a strong benchmark: annual written premium times exposure, scaled to each line's level, scores fold B D² 0.1125 against the champion's 0.1355 (fold A 0.1142 against 0.1280). Second, a quick drop test (this review; two seeds, learning rate 0.08, 350 rounds, native categoricals, fold B only, so the magnitudes are indicative and only the signs are the finding):

| Line | With premium | Without premium | Premium ÷ line-year mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor | 0.0677 | 0.0652 | 0.0660 |
| Buildings | 0.0945 | 0.0960 | 0.0954 |
| Contents | 0.0780 | 0.0773 | 0.0763 |

The permutation importances in `reports/features/feature_study.json` (buildings 0.0395, contents 0.013, motor 0.0057) overstate what premium contributes, because sum_insured, elevation_m and the hazard fields can stand in for it when it is removed. Premium inflates about 3 to 5.5 per cent a year (motor mean 538 in 2021 to 585 in 2024 and 601 in 2025; buildings 297 to 348 and 367; contents 125 to 146 and 154), so 2025 rows sit above every training year on the absolute scale. Normalising by the line-year mean was already tried as 2.9 (ledger 11:09, 0.13364, inside noise), and backlog 2.19 repeats it; close 2.19 or reduce it to its rank-within-line-year variant, which 2.9 did not try.

Recommendation. For Part 1, premium is admissible: it is a supplied test field, set at inception, and the tests above find no same-period signal. Measure it under the real model before deciding (loop items 2.26 and 2.27). Drop it from buildings and contents if 2.26 lands inside the 0.002 floor on both folds, which the quick test suggests it will. Keep it on motor only if removing it costs more than the floor on both folds, and disclose in the report that motor premium is the incumbent's experience-rated renewal price and that, in this data, it reflects prior-year claims that were unreported at the renewal date. Outside the competition, premium should not be an input to the risk model: it is an output of the tariff being built. Use it after modelling, for adequacy and dislocation analysis. For Part 2, put a `use_premium` flag in the market config, default off, and rerun the four tests on the new market's premium before turning it on. One more test is worth running before the report is written: regress log premium on the other features and check whether the residual ranks same-year claims better than next-year claims. A residual that predicts only same-year claims would be a leak signature; one that predicts both is latent risk the incumbent sees and the model does not. Three results would change this advice: 2.26 losing more than the floor on property; 2.27 losing more than 0.004 on motor on both folds (the experience signal would then be worth a separate, disclosed feature); or the timing test on the Part 2 data showing a same-period pattern (premium would then be a leak and must go).

### 3.3 Individual and combined variable consideration

- Families. Treat these as one decision each: age (driver_age, customer_age, policyholder_age, years_licensed; ncb_years correlates 0.34), building age (year_built, property_age), tenure (customer_tenure, renewal_count), size and price (sum_insured, written_premium, bedrooms), flood (flood_zone, elevation_m, distance_to_river_km), weather (seven fields, mostly address constants), marketing and admin (campaign_code, marketing_segment, document_preference, marketing_consent, branch_id, email_domain, previous_insurer), history (four `hist_*` plus prior_claims_5y). Report importance and drop tests per family; within a family, report the representative chosen and why (quality, availability, stability).
- Correlated proxies. elevation_m and distance_to_river_km are partly flood_zone; the weather fields are partly an address identifier; premium is partly sum insured (0.91 on contents) and partly prior claims (motor). Removing one member leaves the signal in another. That fits the buildings result: premium has a permutation importance of 0.04, yet the quick drop test scores slightly better without it.
- SHAP and permutation caveats. With exact duplicates, credit is split arbitrarily and SHAP interaction pairs between duplicates are meaningless. Use conditional permutation (permute within bins of the family representative), family-level drop tests and ALE for the top fields. Repeat permutations at least five times and report a range.
- Interaction screening. The XGBoost pair list (`feature_study.py:270-297`) is built on the top 15 features by one permutation run, with codes for categoricals. Screen candidate pairs on families, confirm with a drop test of an `interaction_constraints` model that forbids the pair, and only then promote. Owner monotone constraints already encode some expected shapes.
- Stability across folds and seeds. Every variable claim in the report should come from both folds and at least three seeds, with sign consistency recorded.
