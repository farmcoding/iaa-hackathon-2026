# Project charter (owner item 3.35)

One page, written before Part 2 so the same frame carries over to a new market. It
answers what the project is for, who uses it, what data and constraints bound it, and
how success is judged, for both the competition entry (Part 1) and a real tariff built
the same way (Part 2 and beyond). Stage 0 and Stage 1 of the pipeline review (section
2.3 of `docs/pipeline_review.md`) had no single artefact before this one; the feature
registry generated by `scripts/feature_registry.py` is the companion stage-0 record,
one row per candidate column.

## Purpose

Predict expected ultimate net claim cost for a policy period, by line, so the cost can
be compared against a price. In the competition this is scored against a held-out
target; in a real tariff the same prediction becomes the risk premium that a rate
structure is built from. The project is a personal entry by a Fellow of the Canadian
Institute of Actuaries and is run and documented against CIA professional standards
(`docs/governance.md`), because the same discipline is owed to a real tariff.

## Scope

- In scope for Part 1: motor, buildings and contents, 2021 to 2024 training data,
  2025 prediction rows, Tweedie D² (power 1.5) against a constant-rate baseline,
  pooled across lines, matching the Kaggle leaderboard metric.
- In scope for Part 2: the same pipeline applied to a new market's data, with a
  different schema, different admissible fields, and a likely thinner and less mature
  claims history. `configs/market_dev.yaml` and `docs/charter.md` are written to be
  re-filled for that market rather than rebuilt from scratch.
- Out of scope: rate-structure design (base rates, discount and loading tables,
  regulatory filing), reinsurance, and anything about the incumbent's own tariff
  beyond the admissibility question in section 2.1 of `docs/pipeline_review.md`.

## Users

- The owner (FCIA), who sets direction, owns every actuarial assumption, and signs
  off every submission (`docs/SIGNOFF.md`).
- The experiment agent, scoped to one file (`src/iaa/experiment.py`) under
  `program.md`; it proposes and tests model changes and never submits.
- The competition judges, who score the final report, video and repo against the
  criteria in `RECOMMENDATION.md` section 1.
- A hypothetical Part 2 pricing team, standing in for whoever would use this pipeline
  on a real tariff: this charter and the registry are written for them too.

## Data

- Host-supplied: policy periods by line (`policies_<line>`, `test_<line>`), claims
  (`claims`), customers, addresses and weather, 2021 to 2025. Loaded and cleaned in
  `src/iaa/data.py`; defects and their treatment are in `reports/data_quality.md`.
- Derived: developed and trended targets (`src/iaa/target.py`, `src/iaa/ultimate.py`),
  the feature frame (`src/iaa/features.py`), and whatever the experiment file builds
  from it inside `fit_predict`.
- Data stays on this machine: it is not redistributed (competition rule 2.5.a) and is
  excluded from git (`data/`, `*.parquet`).

## Constraints

- Permitted inputs: a column is a candidate feature only if it is not in
  `features.FORBIDDEN` (a target-derived or train-only leak) and not one of the
  harness's own bookkeeping columns (`experiment.NON_FEATURES`). Admissibility beyond
  that, whether a field is legitimate to price on as opposed to merely leak-free,
  is a separate, per-field judgement call. `docs/feature_registry.md` and `.csv` record
  it column by column, and section 2.1 and the stage-1 row of section 2.3 in
  `docs/pipeline_review.md` record the reasoning for the contested ones (written
  premium, email_domain, demographic fields, realised weather on the test rows,
  sales/admin fields).
- Owner decisions already made (full list and dates in `reports/assumptions_log.md`):
  development method and IBNER treatment, loss-cost trend by line (and by peril group
  for buildings), level anchoring, the validation scorer and its fold weights, the
  keep rule and its noise floor, library approvals, and the removal of written premium
  and licence points from property, and of written premium from motor.
- The experiment agent may edit only `src/iaa/experiment.py`; data loading, target
  construction, development, trend, folds, scoring and the harness are frozen
  (`program.md`). Every random choice must derive from `SEED_OFFSET` or `SEEDS` so a
  candidate keep can be confirmed on fresh seeds.
- No network access and no new packages for the agent; only the libraries already
  licence-checked and listed in `program.md`.
- Deployment: a real tariff would need a base-rate and loading structure on top of
  this risk premium, rate filing, and ongoing monitoring; none of that is built here.

## Success criteria

- Part 1: the primary validation score (weighted mean fold D², `harness.primary_of`)
  beats the best kept run by more than the noise floor on a fresh-seed confirmation,
  the worse fold does not fall by more than the fold floor, and the selected
  submission is chosen by the rule declared in advance (best fold-B D², ties broken by
  primary then by the worse fold; `reports/assumptions_log.md`, 2026-10-20).
- Judging: performance and adaptation (40%), effective and verified use of AI (25%),
  trust, validation and guardrails (20%), communication and documentation (15%); see
  `RECOMMENDATION.md` section 1 and the mapping in `docs/governance.md`.
- Part 2: the same pipeline runs on the new market with only config and, where
  genuinely needed, experiment-file changes; the `use_premium` flag
  (`configs/market_dev.yaml`, owner item 3.30) and the feature registry are the two
  artefacts meant to make that re-run fast rather than a rebuild.
- Guardrails: the data-contract tests in `tests/test_data_contract.py` (owner item
  3.28) and the ledger's `ae_flag` (owner item 3.23, `src/iaa/harness.py`) hold on
  every run; a failing guardrail test stops the loop before a bad run is scored.

## Governance
